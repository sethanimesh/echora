# ASR experiment decision — 2026-08-15

## Scope

This is a three-utterance engineering smoke test on one speaker, not a model
quality claim. Scores are literal ASR scores before semantic cleanup, personal
context, or the Echora message candidate ranker.

References:

1. `I want water`
2. `I water`
3. `I want water please`

The clips already contain substantial low-energy regions. The estimated maximum
internal gaps are 0.90, 1.02, and 0.34 seconds respectively. These are relative
energy descriptors, not clinical voice-activity annotations.

## Initial public dysarthria-model screen

Four public TORGO/dysarthria fine-tunes were first run locally on the three
personal clips. These literal outputs are retained because they demonstrate
that a model being trained on TORGO does not by itself make it reliable for a
new speaker:

| Model | Clip 1 | Clip 2 | Clip 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dbarbera/whisper-small-torgo-dysarthria-lora` | `water` | `tie bottom` | `I want water please` |
| `macarious/torgo_xlsr_finetune_M04` | `one forti` | `blitter` | `i poned witderlis` |
| `ruch9265/distil-whisper-torgo` | `one` | `high water` | `I want what of these` |
| `sqrk/torgo-mms1ball-Nov29` | `<unk> wornd water` | `<unk>goden` | `<unk> want water please.` |

This screen is too small to rank foundations, but it ruled out treating a
community TORGO checkpoint as a safe starting answer. The later controlled
gate therefore returned to official foundation weights. No candidate ranker or
semantic correction was used to make these outputs look better.

## Frozen zero-shot result

| Decoder | WER | CER | Exact utterances |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Parakeet-TDT 0.6B v3 | 22.2% | 15.8% | 2/3 |
| Parakeet-CTC 1.1B | 44.4% | 34.2% | 1/3 |
| Qwen3-ASR 1.7B | 33.3% | 31.6% | 2/3 |

TDT recognized the first and third messages exactly after normalization and
returned `Hi Gottam` for `I water`. CTC recognized the third, returned
`i more waterar` for the first, and `ny gardon` for the second.

Qwen3-ASR also recognized the first and third messages exactly, but returned
the fluent hallucination `Hi. Good morning.` for `I water`. This is a literal
ASR failure, not an output that may be repaired before scoring.

### Local TORGO preparation completed

The cleaned head-microphone manifest contains 7,941 utterances (6.55 hours):
2,883 from eight dysarthric speakers and 5,058 from seven control speakers.
Duplicate array microphones, non-ASR vocal instructions, and image-prompted
recordings without literal transcripts were excluded. Eight outer folds were
created so each dysarthric speaker is held out once. Each fold also has a
stricter train/development pair with all prompts spoken by its test speaker
removed.

Conservative speed, internal-pause, gain, and noise augmentation code exists
for training audio. The pause transform targets a low-energy internal boundary
and preserves the transcript. It has not been applied to clean development or
test scores, and generated audio is not treated as a new speaker. The prepared
TORGO control speech is useful for retention measurement, but it does not
replace a broader normal-English retention dataset for the later universal-ASR
pilot.

## Controlled pause stress

The same low-energy boundary in each clip was lengthened by 0.5, 1.0, and 2.0
seconds. Clean scores and synthetic stress scores are kept separate.

| Added pause | TDT WER | CTC WER | Qwen3-ASR WER |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0 s | 22.2% | 44.4% | 33.3% |
| 0.5 s | 22.2% | 22.2% | 33.3% |
| 1.0 s | 22.2% | 55.6% | 33.3% |
| 2.0 s | 22.2% | 55.6% | 55.6% |

The flat TDT WER hides a change at two seconds: the difficult second clip falls
from two substitutions to one substitution plus one deletion (`Hi`). CTC is
non-monotonic on this tiny sample, but becomes clearly worse at one and two
seconds. The third utterance remains exact for both decoders at every duration.
This suggests pauses interact with ambiguous acoustics; pauses alone do not
explain every failure.

Qwen3-ASR is unchanged through one added second because it repeats the same
fluent hallucination on the second clip. At two seconds it also changes the
first clip from `I want water` to `Five, one, water`. The third clip remains
exact. This is a smoke-test warning about decoder language priors, not a basis
for rejecting Qwen before the speaker-balanced TORGO foundation gate.

## Speaker-balanced foundation gate

The official untouched `Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-hf` and
`nvidia/parakeet-tdt-1.1b` checkpoints were evaluated on the identical fixed
400-utterance screen: 50 duration-stratified utterances from each of the eight
dysarthric TORGO speakers. Both result files contain exactly 400 unique IDs.
The Qwen run used CPU and the Parakeet run used an L40S, so their recorded RTFs
must not be treated as a fair speed comparison.

| Metric | Qwen3-ASR 1.7B | Parakeet-TDT 1.1B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Speaker-macro WER | **41.90%** | 45.83% |
| Micro WER | **41.76%** | 45.79% |
| Micro CER | **26.48%** | 28.66% |
| Deletion rate | **2.30%** | 5.84% |
| Empty-output rate | **0.00%** | 1.25% |
| Worst-speaker WER | 86.82% | **82.95%** |

Qwen's speaker-macro WER is 8.57% relatively lower, exceeding the frozen 5%
screening threshold while passing both deletion and empty-output guardrails.
On paired utterances, Qwen made fewer word errors on 83, Parakeet on 41, and
they tied on 276; Qwen made 42 fewer word errors in total. Qwen was better on
six speakers and worse on M02 and M04. The M04 regression and the very high
worst-speaker WER remain explicit risks for adaptation and evaluation.

The machine-readable comparison is
`results/foundation_gate/comparison.json`. The raw 800-row concurrent cloud
output was preserved remotely, its duplicate pairs were confirmed to have
identical references and transcripts, and the downloaded Parakeet result was
deduplicated to one row for each of the 400 IDs before comparison.

## Qwen adaptation feasibility findings

The locally cached official model documentation and native Transformers
implementation were inspected before designing a training run. The official
training interface puts the target transcript in the assistant turn as
`language English<asr_text>...`, calls the processor with
`output_labels=True`, and obtains the supervised loss directly from
`Qwen3ASRForConditionalGeneration`. The local benchmark environment has native
Qwen3-ASR support in Transformers 5.15.0. This means the first probe does not
need an unofficial trainer or a conversion to another architecture.

The pinned 1.7B configuration contains:

- a 24-layer audio encoder with width 1,024, feed-forward width 4,096, and
  output width 2,048;
- a multimodal projector between the audio encoder and text model; and
- a 28-layer Qwen3 language decoder with hidden width 2,048.

The available module boundaries allow the language decoder and lower acoustic
layers to remain frozen while selected upper acoustic layers and the
multimodal projector are updated. The proposed first configuration is the last
four audio layers plus the projector, with the language decoder frozen. This
is a proposal for the bounded trainability probe, not a completed or successful
fine-tune. It must be frozen in a config file and reviewed before paid compute.

The official repository also provides full supervised fine-tuning, but the
small size and limited speaker diversity of TORGO make a full-model first run
too prone to memorization and general-speech regression. Partial tuning is
therefore the first experiment; it is not a claim that partial tuning alone
will solve universal stroke-survivor ASR.

## Training-data issue found before gate 2

The proposed M04-held-out fold keeps M04, Qwen's worst foundation-gate speaker,
out of training and uses F03 for development. Its nominal dysarthric training
partition contains 2,044 utterances from F01, F04, M01, M02, M03, and M05.

One nominal training row requires a decision before any "longest example"
memory test: `torgo-M01-Session2_3-0180` is 179.943 seconds long but has only
the prompt transcript `hill`. Approximately 80 seconds are above -60 dB in a
simple 20 ms frame-energy check, so this is not merely a three-minute silent
tail. The prompt cannot be assumed to be a literal transcript of the complete
recording. Feeding it to supervised ASR as one occurrence of `hill` could
teach deletions and make the memory probe test an annotation outlier instead
of the intended speech model.

This row has not been silently removed. Before gate 2, it must either be
listened to and given a literal transcript/segmentation, or be explicitly
excluded by a frozen duration/data-quality rule. If a 30-second eligibility
ceiling is approved for the mechanics probe, the longest eligible dysarthric
row is 20.85 seconds. Long pauses remain a separate required stress condition;
an annotation mismatch is not valid pause augmentation.

## Current experiment state

- Foundation gate 1 is complete and selects Qwen3-ASR 1.7B provisionally.
- No Qwen backward pass has yet been run.
- No Qwen adapter, partial-tuning checkpoint, or fine-tuned weight currently
  exists.
- The Gate 2 objective is still a bounded L40S mechanics test: longest eligible
  example forward/backward, falling loss on a tiny fixed subset, checkpoint
  save, fresh reload, and at least one resumed optimizer step.
- Gate 2 must stop on batch-one OOM, non-finite loss, insufficient loss
  decrease, or failed checkpoint reload. It must not silently switch model,
  GPU, dataset, module selection, or training scope.
- A longer pilot remains unauthorized until Gate 2 passes. Before that pilot,
  a normal-English retention set and frozen speaker-disjoint evaluation
  manifests are required.

## Decision

- Select official Qwen3-ASR 1.7B as the provisional fine-tuning foundation. It
  passed the predeclared speaker-macro WER and deletion/empty-output gate.
- Do not spend the first training budget on Parakeet-TDT 1.1B. Preserve its
  result as a strong transducer baseline and revisit it only if the Qwen
  forward/backward/checkpoint probe fails or Qwen adaptation fails the frozen
  acceptance gates.
- Keep CTC as a required comparator, an alignment/posterior source, and a
  possible future auxiliary objective. It is not the primary model and is not
  part of the candidate ranker.
- Fine-tune foundation weights rather than train from scratch. Start with a
  short Qwen forward/backward, tiny-overfit, checkpoint, reload, and resume
  probe before authorizing a longer run.
- The first adaptation must reach Qwen's acoustic encoder and multimodal
  projector. Decoder-only tuning is not accepted as evidence that dysarthric
  acoustics were learned.
- Never report a random utterance split on TORGO. Report all eight dysarthric
  held-out speakers and also the stricter prompt-disjoint condition.
- Keep synthetic pause audio training-only or in a separately labelled stress
  benchmark. Never merge it into the clean test score.
- Keep the message candidate ranker outside this workstream. ASR alternatives
  must be raw decoder hypotheses; top-1 WER remains the primary score.
- Treat prompt-only labels on unusually long recordings as a data-integrity
  problem, not automatically as examples of long-paused speech. Any exclusion,
  resegmentation, or duration ceiling must be recorded before training.

TORGO alone cannot validate a universal stroke-survivor model: it contains only
about 6.55 usable head-mic hours after cleaning, 2,883 dysarthric utterances,
eight dysarthric speakers, repeated read prompts, and diagnoses that are not a
representative stroke cohort. A TORGO fine-tune is the pipeline proof. A
universal claim requires broader dysarthria causes, real spontaneous messages,
multiple accents/recording environments, and held-out stroke survivors.
